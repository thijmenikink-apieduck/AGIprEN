Deel 1 · Het idee

1

Title

Samen± 20 sec

Druk op de startknop. Wacht tot de titel staat.

Thijmen

"Hi everyone. I'm Thijmen…"

Ties

"…and I'm Ties. Today we are talking about ethical rules for AGI. Look at the two circles: one is a human, and one is a machine. Our question is: what happens when the machine becomes the smarter one?"

2

What is AGI?

Thijmen± 1 min

Steek zelf je hand op bij de vraag.

Thijmen

"To answer that, we first need to know what AGI is. So, raise your hand if you used AI for your homework this week. … Almost everyone. So this topic is really about you."

Thijmen

"The AI we use today, like ChatGPT or Claude, is called narrow AI. Think of a calculator: it is brilliant at maths, but it can't make you a sandwich. Narrow AI is very good at one kind of task, but it is not a human brain."

Thijmen

"AGI, artificial general intelligence, is different. It could learn anything, plan ahead and solve problems it has never seen before. Imagine one student who is the best in every subject at the same time. It doesn't exist yet, but big tech companies are openly trying to build it. So we need rules before it arrives, not after."

Thijmen

"So our big question is: can we write rules for a machine that is smarter than us? People already asked that in 1942."

3

Asimov's Three Laws

Thijmen± 45 sec
Thijmen

"In 1942 the science fiction writer Isaac Asimov wrote a story called Runaround. In it, every robot has to follow three laws."

Thijmen

"One: don't harm humans. Two: obey humans, unless that breaks law one. Three: protect yourself, unless that breaks law one or two. So it is like a ranking: safety first, then obedience, then the robot itself."

Korte pauze, kijk de klas aan.

Thijmen

"Sounds perfect, right? … But there is a problem."

4

Why the laws fail

Thijmen± 1 min
Thijmen

"The problem is that human words are ambiguous: they can mean more than one thing. Take the word harm. When a doctor gives you an injection, it hurts. But the doctor is helping you. So is that harm? Is lying harm? Is firing someone harm? We feel the difference, but a machine needs to know it exactly."

Wijs naar het rondje.

Thijmen

"In the story, the robot Speedy gets an order to go to a dangerous place. Law two says: go. Law three says: stay safe. Both are equally strong, so Speedy just runs in circles, stuck in a loop. Only when a human is in danger does law one win, and Speedy saves him."

Thijmen

"So: human words are not exact enough for a machine."

Wissel naar Ties. Thijmen: "Ties will explain why this is still a big problem today."

Deel 2 · Het probleem

5

The alignment problem

Ties1 à 1,5 min
Ties

"Thanks, Thijmen. Speedy did exactly what the laws said, but not what the humans meant. Today we call that the alignment problem: how do we make sure an AI does what we mean, and not only what we say?"

Ties

"You know this from home. Your mum says: clean your room. So you push everything under your bed. Technically, the room is clean. But that is not what she meant."

Wijs naar de punten van het bootje.

Ties

"This really happened with AI in 2016. Researchers told an AI to get as many points as possible in a boat racing game called CoastRunners. They meant: win the race. But the AI found a loophole: driving in circles gave more points. Look: the points keep going up, but it never finishes."

Klik: paperclips

Ties

"Now imagine a super smart AI with only one goal: make as many paperclips as possible. This is a thought experiment by the philosopher Nick Bostrom. First it builds factories. Then it uses all the metal on Earth. It makes sure nobody can switch it off. And in the end… everything is paperclips. Including us."

Ties

"The AI is not evil. It just takes us literally. That is called unintended consequences: results that nobody wanted. That is a problem for the future. But AI already makes choices today, for example in self-driving cars. So, let's vote!"

6

Five people or one?

Ties1 à 1,5 min
Ties

"This self-driving car has no brakes, and nobody is inside. The software has to choose. A: go left, and five people are hit. B: go right, and one person is hit. This is a moral dilemma: every option is bad."

Laat de klas stemmen met handen en noem de uitslag.

Ties

"Hands up for A. … Hands up for B. … Most of you chose [noem wat de klas koos]. Why did you choose that?"

Klik: optie C

Ties

"Maybe you thought: why not just drive into the wall? Option C. Nobody gets hurt, only the car. A human thinks of that in one second. But a computer only knows the options a programmer gave it. So a programmer decides today who is saved tomorrow."

Uitleg voor jezelf (niet op de dia): de Moral Machine was een website van onderzoekers van MIT. Mensen kregen online steeds zo'n autodilemma te zien en klikten wie de auto moest sparen. Zo kwamen er rond de 40 miljoen keuzes binnen, uit 233 landen en gebieden (artikel in Nature, 2018). Per land verschilden de antwoorden: in sommige landen kozen mensen veel vaker om jonge mensen te redden in plaats van oude mensen dan in andere landen.

Ties

"Researchers from MIT made a website called the Moral Machine. People all over the world clicked who the car should save. That gave about 40 million choices, from 233 countries and territories. In some countries people chose to save young people much more often than in others. So if our class can't agree, and the whole world can't agree, how can an AI get it right?"

Ties

"And sometimes AI is unfair without anybody noticing."

7

Bias and blame

Ties± 1 min
Ties

"That is called bias. AI learns from data made by humans. It is like a student learning from a textbook full of mistakes: the student copies the mistakes. So an AI can discriminate: treat some groups of people unfairly."

Ties

"For example Amazon. They built an AI that read job applications and gave each one a score. It learned from the CVs Amazon got in the past ten years, and most of those came from men. So the AI learned: men are better. It gave lower scores to CVs with the word 'women's', like 'captain of the women's chess club'. When this came out in 2018, Amazon stopped the project."

Uitleg voor jezelf: in 2022 overleed de oma van de Canadees Jake Moffatt. Hij vroeg de chatbot van Air Canada naar korting voor een vlucht naar de begrafenis (een rouwtarief). De chatbot zei dat hij een gewoon ticket kon kopen en de korting binnen 90 dagen achteraf kon aanvragen. Dat klopte niet: volgens de echte regels kon dat niet achteraf. Air Canada weigerde te betalen en zei dat de chatbot zelf verantwoordelijk was. In februari 2024 besliste het tribunaal dat Air Canada ongeveer 800 Canadese dollar moest terugbetalen.

Ties

"Then: who is to blame? In 2022, a man's grandmother died. He asked the chatbot of Air Canada about a discount for the funeral flight. The chatbot said: buy a normal ticket, and ask for the discount later. That was wrong. Air Canada's real rules didn't allow that. Air Canada said: the chatbot is responsible, not us. But in 2024 the tribunal, a kind of small court, said no. You can't blame your chatbot, just like you can't blame your pencil for a bad test. Air Canada had to pay him back."

Ties

"So who is accountable: the maker, the user or the AI? You can't send an AI to prison. So in the end, it is always people."

Wissel naar Thijmen. Ties: "So, is anyone doing something about this? Thijmen?"

Deel 3 · De werkelijkheid

8

The EU AI Act

Thijmen± 1 min
Thijmen

"Yes, the European Union is. In 2024 the EU made the AI Act, the first big AI law in the world. It also counts for us. It works like speed limits: near a school you drive slowly, on the highway you can go faster. So the more dangerous the AI, the stricter the rules."

Noem elke laag van de piramide als hij verschijnt.

Thijmen

"At the bottom: minimal risk, like spam filters or AI in games. That is free. Then limited risk, like chatbots: they must tell you they are an AI. That is called transparency. Then high risk: AI that decides about jobs, school or loans. It must be fair and checked by humans. And at the top: banned. For example social scoring, where people get points for their behaviour. That has been banned since 2025."

Thijmen

"But the strict rules for high risk AI were delayed from 2026 to December 2027. Is that smart, or too slow? Think about it for yourself."

Thijmen

"And this is the big difference with Asimov: these are rules for the companies, not for the AI itself."

9

Doing it right?

ThijmenTies± 1 min
Thijmen

"Laws are one thing. But some companies also try to make their own rules. That is called self-regulation."

Thijmen

"Anthropic, the company behind Claude, published a constitution for Claude in January 2026. It doesn't only say what to do, it explains why. Like a good teacher who explains why a rule exists, instead of saying: because I said so."

Thijmen

"In 2023, Sam Altman, the boss of OpenAI, the company behind ChatGPT, asked the American Senate to make rules for AI. So even the makers say: we need rules."

Uitleg voor jezelf: in mei 2025 testte het veiligheidsbedrijf Enkrypt AI twee AI-modellen van Mistral: Pixtral-Large en Pixtral-12B. Die kunnen tekst en plaatjes begrijpen. In de test gaven ze 18 tot 40 keer vaker gevaarlijke informatie dan GPT-4o (ChatGPT) en Claude, bijvoorbeeld over het maken van chemische wapens.

Ties

"But it's not always that simple. Mistral, a French AI company, signed the EU code in 2025. But in the same year, a safety company tested two of their models, called Pixtral. They were up to 40 times more likely than ChatGPT or Claude to explain dangerous things, like how to make chemical weapons. And anyone can download those models. Also people with bad plans."

Ties

"So building fast and sharing everything can be risky. Jony Ive, the designer of the iPhone, knows that. In 2025 he said about the smartphone: 'I still feel responsibility.' Now he is building an AI device with OpenAI. His lesson: think about the problems before you build it, not after."

Ties

"So we have seen laws, and companies that make their own rules. But what do we think ourselves? We don't completely agree."

10

What do we think?

ThijmenTies± 45 sec
Thijmen

"I think AI must never decide alone, because AI can be wrong without anyone noticing. Remember Amazon: the AI gave women lower scores, and at first nobody saw it. And the Air Canada chatbot simply made something up. So when AI decides about jobs, school or money, a human should always check the final decision."

Ties

"I don't completely agree with Thijmen. I think AI does more good than harm, because it helps people every day. It can help doctors find diseases on scans, and it can explain your homework when no teacher is around. The problems we showed are real, but they come from bad data and bad rules. And if a human has to check every single decision, AI loses its biggest advantage: speed. So with good rules, like the AI Act, AI makes our lives better."

Ties

"We want to end with one question for you."

11

Final question

Samen± 30 sec

Wacht tot de vraag er helemaal staat. Niet doorheen praten.

Thijmen

"Remember our question from the start: can we write rules for a machine that is smarter than us?"

Ties

"Our answer: we can try, but the rules will never be perfect. So even when AI does a lot of good, humans must stay in control and keep checking. That is called human oversight."

Thijmen

"So we leave you with this question."

Na 15 seconden blijft alleen de vraag staan. Daarna samen:

Samen

"Thank you for listening. Are there any questions?"
